LCM One robot at a time · robots see only which nodes are occupied

Robots stand on the nodes of a ring. Each one sees which nodes are occupied, but not how many robots share a node, and has no sense of left or right. They take turns in a fixed order they do not know; on its turn a robot stays or steps to a neighbouring node. Every start gathers on one node within n − 3 rounds, except the few starts no algorithm can gather, which the page flags.

How the robots decide

Every robot follows the same rules and remembers nothing from one turn to the next. On its turn it looks at the ring and sees only which nodes are occupied: it cannot count the robots on a node, and it cannot tell clockwise from anticlockwise, so it describes what it sees by the empty stretches between occupied nodes (the gaps) and by which of its two neighbours are occupied.

The robots take turns in a fixed order that they do not know. A round ends once every robot has had its turn. On its turn a robot either stays or steps to one of its two neighbouring nodes.

The rules are checked from the most finished situation down to the most spread out one, and the first that matches decides the move. Gathered robots stay. When two or three neighbouring nodes are left, the robots at the ends step onto the occupied neighbour. When two occupied nodes have one empty node between them, robots step into it. With several gaps, only robots standing next to one of the biggest gaps move, which pulls the robots together a little more every round.

From almost every start the robots end up on a single node within n − 3 rounds, where n is the number of nodes. A few starts are symmetric in a way no rule can break, because the turn order can always undo what one robot does; the page marks those starts so you can watch them fail.
